# Trade Masters

Use this file as the living registry of known trade masters, vocabulary, and strategy families. Update this file when repeated examples reveal stable language or recurring setups.

## Elite Swing Traders

### Vocabulary

- `fake break`: a failed or premature breakout attempt that does not fully damage the larger bullish structure
- `squat`: a constructive pause or compression near support, often near a rising moving average or prior support shelf
- `breakout`: renewed expansion through a pivot, range high, or resistance area after the setup resets
- `pivot on the monthly chart off the 50`: a higher-timeframe reversal or support reaction from the monthly 50-period moving average
- `DTR`: daily downtrend line or daily trendline resistance that price is reclaiming or breaking
- `base resistance`: the upper boundary of a base or consolidation range
- `change in character`: an observable shift from weak or corrective behavior to constructive strength
- `base breakout`: price expansion through the top of the base after the character change is underway
- `snipe`: an early, favorable-risk entry taken before the move becomes obvious, often near a pivot, reclaim, or pre-breakout inflection
- `R/R off the charts`: unusually attractive risk/reward because the entry is tight relative to the upside if the setup resolves
- `clustered MA's`: short and intermediate moving averages compressed together, often signaling an inflection or trend reset if they begin to align bullishly
- `starting to cross back over`: moving averages beginning to flip back into bullish alignment after weakness or consolidation
- `clear this 75 level resistance`: reclaim or break a clearly identified horizontal price level that is acting as overhead resistance
- `huge move out of the base`: strong expansion once price resolves above the base resistance
- `buying gap support pivots`: entering as price pivots constructively from support created by a prior gap, usually because the support offers a tight invalidation point
- `best R/R entry`: an entry with unusually favorable risk/reward because support is clearly defined and nearby
- `buying earnings gap ups when they pullback to gap support`: a preferred subtype of the gap-support trade where the original gap is driven by earnings and the pullback retests that support
- `buying earnings gap support is one of the best R/R entry tactics you will come across`: a high-conviction master-level belief that earnings-gap support entries are among the best asymmetric long-entry tactics
- `triggered but more importantly the gap support entry`: the later confirmation matters, but the real edge was the earlier support-based entry with tighter risk
- `know the levels and trade them accordingly`: identify the exact support or trigger zones in advance and use them to control entry, stop, and trade management
- `8ema`: the 8-period exponential moving average used as fast dynamic support or resistance
- `gap support/ 8ema`: a combined support zone where the pullback is expected to hold both the earnings-gap support area and the rising 8 EMA
- `trades a little thin`: liquidity is lower than ideal, so execution may be less forgiving even if the setup is attractive
- `low 17mil float`: low share float is part of the stock's personality and may amplify expansion or volatility
- `earnings flag`: a tight continuation pattern forming after an earnings-driven gap or impulse move
- `tight as a drum`: unusually tight consolidation, often implying pressure build-up before continuation
- `first pullback after the new ATH`: the first constructive retracement after price makes a new all-time high
- `tested the 50ema`: price retraces into the 50 EMA and successfully holds or reclaims it
- `inside day`: a session contained within the prior day's range, often signaling contraction before a move
- `buy RS on weakness`: favor names whose relative strength holds up even while price pulls back or the market is weak
- `8 week support pivots`: higher-timeframe support reactions where price tests the 8-week support zone and turns constructively higher, often in a stock that has already shown leadership
- `had all the ingredients I look for`: a canonical example containing the preferred checklist for the setup
- `squat to flush out support`: a brief shakeout or pressure event that forces out weak support holders before the setup resolves higher
- `real move`: the true continuation phase that follows after the fake break and squat have done their work
- `perfect example`: a chart the master considers a textbook version of the setup
- `up on well above average volume`: a strong up day with volume materially above normal, often implying institutional participation
- `highest up day volume since last April reversal`: unusually strong upside participation relative to an important historical reference point
- `both gapping up this morning`: broad market follow-through is continuing into the next session via opening strength
- `QQQ & SPY`: major index ETFs used as proxies for overall market tone and participation
- `heading back for support`: price is likely rotating down toward a known support area rather than immediately resuming higher
- `every rally into declining MA's continues to be sold`: rallies are failing at downward-sloping moving averages, signaling persistent supply and weak tape character
- `declining MA's`: falling moving averages that act as dynamic resistance during a downtrend or corrective phase
- `EMA crossover`: a moving-average crossover used as a regime signal rather than a one-bar event
- `on the weekly`: the signal is intended as a higher-timeframe condition and should carry more regime weight than a daily fluctuation
- `rolling over`: price action and trend structure are turning down after weakness rather than stabilizing
- `careful out there`: a cautionary market-regime warning rather than a specific entry instruction
- `lost the 200sma`: price has broken below the 200-day simple moving average, weakening longer-term trend support
- `MA's are running downhill`: moving averages are sloping lower together, implying deteriorating trend structure after prior weakness
- `after the EMA crossover`: the deterioration is happening after a bearish moving-average crossover, increasing the weight of the warning
- `bear flag`: a weak countertrend consolidation that often resolves lower in a bearish environment
- `the more times we test the level the more likely it will fail`: repeated tests weaken support and increase breakdown risk
- `conditions are deteriorating rapidly`: tape quality is worsening quickly rather than stabilizing
- `gapping down near the 200sma`: downside pressure is arriving directly into an important long-term support level
- `buying RS names on weakness continues to be my best trades`: a core master preference indicating that relative-strength names bought on controlled weakness should receive extra attention and weighting

- `RS Rating`: rank-style relative-performance score, distinct from the plotted RS line
- `RS new high before price`: the relative-strength line leads by making a new high before price fully does, signaling leadership

- `turning old support into new resistance`: a formerly supportive level is now acting as overhead supply after being lost
- `struggle to clear the 200`: inability to reclaim the 200 SMA is the actionable evidence that the bounce is failing
- `look for short entry`: the setup is not just cautionary context; it is a conditional short idea if resistance holds
- `all of my best trades start with a post earnings consolidation`: the master's highest-conviction longs often begin with orderly tightening after an earnings-driven gap or impulse
- `turn into continuations`: the preferred post-earnings structure is not reversal hunting but constructive continuation after consolidation
- `most big moves start with a gap up`: earnings or catalyst gaps are treated as the ignition point for many of the master’s favorite expansion trades
- `that's where I stay focused`: the master is explicitly prioritizing gap-up and post-earnings continuation structures over more miscellaneous setups
- `tops are a process`: market tops are interpreted as gradual exhaustion phases rather than isolated one-bar events
- `rounding shape`: a curved topping structure showing progressive loss of upside momentum
- `head and shoulders`: an alternate topping structure expressing repeated failed attempts to make further upside progress
- `gradual exhaustion of buyers`: demand weakens over time even while price may still appear stable near highs
- `breadth begins to thin`: fewer individual stocks participate in the index strength, signaling internal deterioration
- `slow rolling over`: the market weakens progressively before any dramatic break, rather than collapsing all at once
- `golden rule of situational awareness`: market environment should override pattern quality when the two conflict
- `the best setup in the wrong environment is a losing trade`: setup selection is subordinate to regime context and tape quality
- `setting up under ATH resistance`: price is consolidating or tightening directly below all-time-high overhead resistance
- `blue skies breakout`: breakout into open price territory with little or no overhead supply once all-time highs are cleared
- `closed at high of day`: the session finishes with strength, suggesting buyers stayed in control into the close and pressure remains pointed upward
- `30 min pivot`: a lower-timeframe pivot used to refine timing and define a tighter-risk entry within a broader swing setup
- `$1.50 risk entry`: the setup is attractive partly because the lower-timeframe trigger keeps the stop distance tight and explicit
- `gapping up over 5% this morning`: strong next-session follow-through confirms the earlier setup and rewards the tight-risk entry

- `fake-out`: a false move beyond a consolidation boundary that lures traders in and then reverses
- `bull/bear trap`: a failed breakout or breakdown that catches traders on the wrong side of the move
- `liquidity grabs`: large players using the surge of breakout or breakdown participation to fill their own orders
- `volume confirmation`: breakout validity improves when volume expands meaningfully above recent average
- `re-test requirement`: waiting for old resistance to become support, or old support to become resistance, before trusting the move
- `closing rule`: the breakout or breakdown is not trusted until the candle closes on the relevant timeframe
- `2% rule`: a move is more trustworthy if price can hold at least modestly beyond the breakout level instead of barely poking through it
- `double inside week`: two consecutive weekly bars contained within the range of the prior reference bar, often signaling compression before expansion
- `sector ETF is setting up`: the trade group itself is becoming actionable and should guide where to look for individual names
- `go find the best charts setting up in the ETF`: once the group setup is present, scan constituents for the strongest aligned charts
- `top down approach`: start with sector or group strength, then drill down into the best individual stocks inside that group
- `when gaps don't backfill that's bullish`: the inability of price to fill a prior gap is itself a bullish continuation sign, showing persistent demand
- `PEG list`: likely shorthand for a curated post-earnings-gap watchlist or framework that the master uses heavily inside the strategy
- `engulfing candle`: a strong reversal or continuation candle whose real body overtakes the prior candle and signals renewed control
- `approaching the next pivot`: price is advancing toward the next nearby resistance or trigger level after a constructive reversal or support hold
- `a whole new crop of setups are on the horizon`: earnings season creates a fresh wave of candidate charts rather than just updating old ones
- `cheat code for finding future leadership`: earnings season is viewed as an efficient way to identify the next leading stocks early

## Sean trades

- `leading stock`: the candidate should already be behaving like a leader rather than a random weak-stock bounce

- `8 EMA`: the rising 8-period exponential moving average acts as dynamic trend support and context for the entry
- `entry`: the buy zone appears to be the reclaim area after the undercut, not the initial breakdown itself
- `stop loss`: risk is defined beneath the undercut low or the reclaimed support zone

### Notes

- This master appears to teach compact, schematic system logic rather than long narrative chart commentary.

- The undercut appears to be a shakeout and reclaim pattern, not a momentum breakout chase. Preserve the reversal-through-support logic.
